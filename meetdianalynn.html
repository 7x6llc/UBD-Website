Meet Diana Lynn

A life that taught her what the work would eventually become.

Diana Lynn, Founder
Unbecoming By Design™

Diana Lynn did not set out to build a coaching practice around the word "Unbecoming." She arrived at it the way most true things are arrived at — slowly, through experience, and only in hindsight.

What She Carried

For seventeen years, Diana was married to a man whose control and volatility shaped nearly every decision in her household. She stayed, in part, because staying was what she had learned a good wife and a responsible mother did. She raised her two sons inside that marriage, and she raised them well — both grew into men who chose lives centered on serving others, one as a teacher and one as a United States Marine.

Alongside that marriage, Diana built a fifteen-year career in corporate America, moving through banking and insurance, learning the discipline of performance, deadlines, and professional composure. She was good at her job. She was good at most of what was asked of her. That, too, was part of the pattern.

Leaving didn't feel like becoming someone new. It felt like finally admitting who she had been underneath the role the whole time.

The Turn

Eventually, Diana left the marriage. In the years that followed, she began helping other women work through the mental and emotional shifts required to move past their own experiences of domestic trauma — not as a therapist, but as someone who understood, firsthand, how disorienting it is to rebuild a sense of self after years of shaping it around someone else's expectations.

That work led her to coaching and personal development more formally. As she studied Human Design, NLP, and trauma-informed approaches to change, she began to recognize a pattern in her own history: the changes that mattered most in her life were rarely about deciding to become someone new. They were about understanding why she had become who she was, honoring what those adaptations had once accomplished, and then deciding — consciously, deliberately — what still belonged.

What Else Changed

Diana's own Unbecoming didn't stop with her marriage. She left the faith tradition she was raised in and chose a spiritual path more aligned with her beliefs, her experience, and her understanding of life. She remarried and became part of a large blended family. She moved across the country. Each of those changes — even the ones she wanted, even the good ones — required releasing identities, expectations, relationships, assumptions, and ways of functioning that had once made complete sense.

That is the part people often miss about positive change: it still asks something of you. It still asks you to let go of who you had to be to get here.

Why This Work

Diana doesn't tell clients that because she went through hard things, she knows how to fix them. That was never the premise. What her life taught her is something more specific: that personal change is rarely a single decision. It is an ongoing practice of understanding why you became who you became, honoring what those adaptations once accomplished, and consciously choosing what still belongs in the life you're building now.

That is the work she brings to every client, every workshop, and every conversation — Unbecoming the expectations. Living by your design.
